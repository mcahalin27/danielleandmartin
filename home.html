Travelling There

We recommend renting a car from Dublin Airport — it's the most straightforward option and gives you the freedom to explore the Irish countryside while you're here. You can also fly into Shannon or Cork, but Dublin is the easiest connection. Gloster House is roughly 1 hour 30 minutes from Dublin, 1 hour from Shannon, and 85 minutes from Galway.

What time does the ceremony start?

The ceremony begins at 3:00pm. Shuttles depart Birr town at 1:45pm — we recommend hopping on the bus so you're seated in plenty of time.

What is the dress code?

Black Tie. We'd love to see everyone dressed to the nines — it's going to be a beautiful evening.

Is there parking at the venue?

Yes, parking is available at Gloster House. Full details to follow closer to the date.

Are children welcome?

We love your little ones, but the evening reception is adults only. More details to follow closer to the date.

Will there be a bus service?

Yes! Shuttles depart Birr at 1:45pm on Friday and the final bus back to Birr leaves at 3:30am. On Day Two, buses to Birr depart Gloster House at 7:00pm. Pick-up point details to follow.

Is there a Day Two?

Absolutely — and we'd love you to join us. BBQ and live music at Gloster House from 2:30pm on Saturday 12 June, followed by Ceol agus Craic at Craughwell's in Birr from 7:30pm.

What is the nearest airport?

Dublin Airport is the most straightforward, roughly 1 hour 30 minutes away. Shannon is about an hour and is a great option for those flying from the US.

What if I can't make it?

We'll miss you! Please let us know as soon as you can so we can plan accordingly.

Ireland's cultural capital. Buzzing streets, brilliant restaurants, trad music in every second pub and the famous Quay Street. Compact, walkable and hard not to love.

A beautiful harbour town wedged between the Atlantic and the mountains. Exceptional seafood, cosy pubs, a working fishing fleet and dramatic scenery in every direction.

Kinsale

Ireland's gourmet capital — a colourful coastal town with excellent restaurants, a pretty harbour and the striking star-shaped Charles Fort just outside.

Kilkenny

A beautifully preserved medieval city with a magnificent castle, a strong craft and design scene and some very good pubs along the Medieval Mile.

A gorgeous Georgian town at the foot of Croagh Patrick on Clew Bay. Great pubs, live music and a jumping-off point for Achill Island.

Kenmare
3 hrs · Co. Kerry

A charming, brightly painted town at the meeting point of the Ring of Kerry and the Ring of Beara. Superb food and a lovely base for exploring the southwest.

🚗 Road Trips
The Wild Atlantic Way
Multi-day · West Coast

Ireland's epic 2,500km coastal route running from Donegal down to Cork. You don't need to drive all of it — even a single day along the Clare and Galway stretch is unforgettable.

Cliffs of Moher & The Burren
1 hr · Co. Clare

Ireland's most iconic natural landmark, with 8km of cliffs rising over 200m above the Atlantic. Pair it with the extraordinary lunar limestone landscape of the Burren.

The Ring of Kerry
2 hrs 30 to reach · Co. Kerry

A classic 179km loop around the Iveragh Peninsula taking in mountains, lakes, beaches and coastline. One of Europe's great drives — allow a full day.

Connemara
2 hrs · Co. Galway

Wild, empty and stunningly beautiful. Bog, mountains and lakes, with Kylemore Abbey and the Twelve Bens. Arguably the most atmospheric landscape in the country.

Clonmacnoise
25 minutes · Co. Offaly

One of Ireland's most important early Christian monastic sites, founded in the 6th century and sitting peacefully on the banks of the River Shannon. Very close to Gloster.

Giant’s Causeway
3 hrs 30 · Co. Antrim

A UNESCO World Heritage Site of 40,000 interlocking basalt columns on the Antrim coast. A long drive but a spectacular one, and easily combined with Belfast.

🍀 Irish Wedding Tips

If this is your first Irish wedding, a few things tend to catch visitors out. Here's what to expect.

Day Two is a real event, not a recovery day

This is the big one for our Australian guests. Day Two is not a quiet debrief over a coffee — it's a full second day of drinking, eating and music that runs from 2:30pm until late. Pace yourself on the Friday accordingly.

Don't book an early Sunday flight

Following on from the above: Saturday night in Birr will go long. A Sunday morning flight out of Dublin means leaving before dawn on very little sleep. Fly Sunday evening or Monday if you possibly can.

The day starts early and finishes very late

A 3pm ceremony and a 3:30am final bus makes for a twelve-hour day. That's completely standard here. Nobody slips away after dinner — staying to the end is the norm, not the exception.

There will be a second dinner

Late in the night, usually around midnight, more food appears — sandwiches, cocktail sausages, sausage rolls, chips. This is a genuine Irish institution and one of the great joys of the night. Leave room.

Speeches come before dinner

Ours are during the drinks reception. Irish weddings often front-load the speeches so everyone can relax afterwards, rather than the American style of speeches between courses.

Take the bus — the limits are strict

Ireland's drink-driving limits are lower than Australia's, the US and much of Europe, and enforcement is serious. There is genuinely no safe number of drinks. That's exactly why we're running coaches both nights.

Rounds are a social contract

In an Irish pub, one person buys drinks for the whole group, then the next person does. Don't offer to pay your share — just get the next round in. Skipping your turn is genuinely noticed.

Pack for four seasons in one day

June averages around 15–19°C and rain is always possible, even in a good week. Bring a layer for the evening and don't rely on the forecast. A wrap or jacket over black tie is completely normal here.

Tipping is modest

Nothing like the US. Around 10–15% in restaurants is generous, and you don't tip at the bar at all. Taxi fares are usually just rounded up.

Practical bits

The Republic uses the euro, not sterling. Plugs are the same three-pin type as the UK at 230V. Driving is on the left, and rural roads are narrower than you're expecting — allow more time than the map suggests.

Being asked to sing is a compliment

Late in the night someone may call for a party piece. There is no expectation of talent whatsoever — enthusiasm counts for everything. Having one song you can get through is a genuinely useful thing to have in reserve.

The band, then the DJ

Live band first, DJ afterwards, running until the small hours. The dance floor is not optional in the way it might be elsewhere — expect everyone from grandparents to small children to be up there.
